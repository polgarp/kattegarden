Kattegården · phase 1

Three ways the yard could look

Same corner of the yard in each: a loaf cat who asked for a bed for 12 kittens, the gassy cat (as promised), a ball of yarn. Only the drawing style changes.

Kladdehæfte notebook doodle

Ballpoint lines on squared maths paper, with marker colour that doesn't quite stay inside the lines. The yard grid is literally the grid on the paper.

Fits her
Feels like her own drawing come alive; pairs naturally with a picture maker later.
Risk
Squared paper may smell of homework, the one thing we're running from.

Klodser chunky toy blocks

Thick outlines, flat bright colours, hard toy shadows, and cushions with studs on top. Everything looks like you could snap it onto something else.

Fits her
Speaks the language of building; slapstick reads well with bold shapes.
Risk
Loudest of the three; cuteness has to come from expressions, not delicacy.

Klistermærker sticker album

Every cat and object is a die-cut sticker on a felt yard. Collecting a cat feels like peeling a sticker for your album.

Fits her
The collection mechanic is the look; arranging stickers is building with a lighter touch.
Risk
Quietest at phone size; jokes need bigger gestures to land.

What to judge

  1. Show her all three without explaining. Which one does she point at, and which cat does she ask about?
  2. At phone size, do the cats read as cute and the gassy cat read as a joke?
  3. Does the 4 × 3 cushion bed read as "a bed for 12" without looking like a worksheet?
  4. Mixing is allowed: "B's cats with C's stickers" is a fine answer.
